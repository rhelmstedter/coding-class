#+title: Madlibs

Madlibs is the follow-up to the [[file:introduction_paragraph.org][Introduction Paragraph]]. It is deliberately the same programming problem wearing a different hat: collect some words with =input()=, drop them into a string with f-strings, print the result. No new syntax at all.

That is the point. Students get a second pass at the same mechanics while their attention is on writing a silly story rather than on remembering how =input()= works. Repetition at the same difficulty level, not a step up.

I have students play an [[https://www.glowwordbooks.com/blog/category/kids-online-mad-libs/][online madlib]] first so they know what they are building.

** Requirements

1. Ask for *5 to 8 words* using =input()=, storing each one in its own variable.
2. Use *at least three different parts of speech* -- for example a noun, a verb, and an adjective. Do not ask for six nouns.
3. *Label every prompt with its part of speech.* =input("A plural noun: ")= tells the user what to type. =input("Word: ")= does not.
4. Print the story using *f-strings*.
5. Every word collected has to show up in the story.
6. Run it at least twice with different words.

The story itself is the student's. Graded on structure -- variable count, parts of speech represented, f-string use -- not on how funny it is.

** If You Finish Early
